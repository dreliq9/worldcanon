# Chapter 1 — Arrival

The ferry shuddered into the harbor of [[Brackwater]] just after dawn,
and [[Lyra Vance]] saw the lighthouse before she saw the town.

It was new. Or new-ish — younger than the cliffs, anyway, paler stone
than her grandmother's house had been. The old lighthouse, the one she
had watched the storm take when she was eight, was gone in the way only
broken things are gone: a foundation, then nothing. The new one stood a
hundred yards south of where the old had stood, as if the headland
itself had decided to forget.

She tightened her grip on her bag. The scar on her left forearm itched,
the way it always did near the sea. Twenty years away, and her body
still remembered.

The dock was quiet. A pair of fishermen, hauling nets, paused to watch
her cross the gangway. Their faces meant nothing to her — too young to
remember the Vance girl who had left at nineteen — but the way they
looked at the lighthouse and then at her was something else. As if they
were measuring her against it.

She tried not to think about [[Stillweaving]]. The lighthouse beam swept
the cliffs, and she could feel it pulling at the place in her chest
where the practice lived. Not gone, then. Suppressed. Different.

"You're Maren Vance's granddaughter," one of the fishermen said. It was
not a question.

Lyra nodded. "I am."

He looked at the lighthouse again, and then at her, and said nothing
more.
